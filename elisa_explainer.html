ELISA: Procedure & Binding Affinity Estimation

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay — principles, protocol, and KD estimation from titration data

What is ELISA?

ELISA is a plate-based immunoassay that uses enzyme-linked antibodies to detect and quantify proteins, antigens, or antibodies in solution. Signal is generated when an enzyme conjugated to a detection antibody converts a colorimetric substrate, producing absorbance proportional to the amount of bound analyte.

Step-by-step procedure

1

Coat

Antigen (or capture Ab) adsorbed to 96-well plate overnight. Unbound material washed away.

2

Block

Free sites saturated with BSA or milk to prevent non-specific binding, then washed.

3

Primary binding

Analyte added in serial dilution series; incubated to allow binding equilibrium.

4

Detection

HRP-conjugated secondary Ab added, binds primary analyte; excess washed off.

5

Signal readout

TMB substrate added → color change → stop reaction → absorbance read at 450 nm.

Figure 2 — Sigmoidal binding curve & EC₅₀

Absorbance vs. concentration data is fitted to a 4-parameter logistic (4PL) sigmoidal model. The EC₅₀ — concentration at half-maximal signal — approximates the apparent KD under dilute conditions.

Reading off KD: the inflection point of the sigmoidal curve (where A₄₅₀ = (Amax + Amin) / 2) gives the EC₅₀. When free analyte ≈ total analyte (dilute regime), EC₅₀ ≈ apparent KD.

Caveats for affinity estimation

Surface immobilizationCoating can alter antigen conformation, affecting binding vs. solution conditions.
Avidity inflationMultivalent binding to immobilized antigen artificially lowers apparent KD vs. true monovalent affinity.
Hook effectAt very high analyte concentrations, signal may decrease — violating the EC₅₀ = KD assumption.
Best use caseELISA affinity estimates are best for rank-ordering binders. Precise KD requires SPR (Biacore) or BLI.
